# PREY — Mission 05: The Autopsy

Date: 19 September 2026

Scope: adversarial review of Missions 01–04. This mission used only the existing PREY record. It did not hunt for new ideas, build, contact anyone, or post publicly.

## Executive diagnosis

PREY has not shown that attractive tiny markets are absent. It has shown that public-web research can reliably find pain, spending, incumbents, and reasons not to act. The process then treats an inexpensive incumbent as proof that no narrow segment can be won, even when that incumbent is also the strongest available proof that buyers recognize and fund the job.

The evidence supports **B, C, D, E, and F more strongly than A**:

- **A — not established.** Twenty-nine distinct candidates across four missions are not a representative test of tiny markets, especially when no user was asked and no offer was exposed.
- **B — established.** Nineteen of 28 killed candidates (68%) were killed primarily by the incumbent/competition-gap gate. Several conclusions establish that a generic tool is unnecessary, not that a specialized service or differently packaged outcome is unsellable.
- **C — established.** Search began in public complaints, GitHub issues, Reddit, product documentation, and public job posts. Problems visible there are unusually likely to have maintainers, workarounds, vendors, or bidders already responding.
- **D — established.** Requiring $0 distribution, a tiny build, proven spending for the exact output, weak incumbents, objective work, and a first dollar in 48 hours creates an intersection so narrow that legitimate service wedges are rejected for involving judgment and legitimate product wedges are rejected for having competitors.
- **E — established.** The hunts were ecosystem-wide and operator-anonymous. They did not begin from an owned audience, demonstrated domain credibility, an existing asset, or a privileged workflow corpus.
- **F — established.** PREY performed four research missions, examined 29 distinct candidates, contacted zero users, made zero offers, and ran zero money tests. Research is now substituting for market contact.

## Status rules used in this autopsy

- **VERIFIED DEATH:** a hard fact defeats the exact proposed output or makes delivery materially unsafe/impractical. It does not necessarily kill all services in the market.
- **RESEARCH-BASED DEATH:** several public facts make the bet unattractive, but no target user tested the conclusion.
- **PREMATURE DEATH:** the decisive claim is an inference about willingness to pay, segmentation, positioning, or human preference that desk research cannot settle.
- **RESERVE / REOPEN:** not a resurrection or approval to build; it means the candidate deserves a bounded exposure test before a final kill.

## 2 — Gate autopsy

Primary fatal gate across the 28 killed candidates:

| Gate | Kills | Share | Diagnosis |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Incumbent / competition gap | 19 | 68% | Disproportionate veto; sometimes a valid product kill, sometimes an untested market kill |
| Monetization / repeated exact demand | 4 | 14% | Generally appropriate, though exact-output proof was demanded more strictly than most early markets can supply |
| Product shape / human judgment | 3 | 11% | Reveals micro-SaaS bias: viable services were rejected because humans create value |
| Feasibility / platform / reach risk | 2 | 7% | Appropriate hard constraint for Cricut and EPC under the tiny, fast, safe requirement |

### The cheap-incumbent assumption

“An inexpensive/free incumbent means the opportunity is dead” is true only when all of the following hold:

1. it serves the same narrow user and starting state;
2. it produces the same accepted outcome, not merely a component;
3. switching/onboarding is easy enough for that user;
4. it handles the segment’s important exceptions;
5. the buyer does not value done-for-you execution, trust, speed, or accountability;
6. there is no reachable segment the incumbent ignores; and
7. PREY has verified those points with users, not only feature pages.

It is false when the incumbent proves demand but leaves a winnable boundary. An offer can win through easier workflow, vertical specialization, done-for-you speed, integration, trust, localization, different pricing, service plus software, or audience-led packaging. Competition is disqualifying when it removes meaningful differentiation; it is validating when buyers recognize the category and the segment still has a reason to switch or delegate.

Payload, OpenRefine, TeamSnap, Pixieset/Lightroom, Superset, and micro-label royalties are credible exact-output deaths. Square payouts, KDP formatting, bodycam SRT correction, Etsy listing setup, and Patreon were treated too broadly. Research proved generic software unattractive; it did not prove a specialized done-for-you outcome unsellable. This does not manufacture survivors: only Square and bodycam SRT have enough current pain/spend evidence to receive REOPEN status now.

## 3 — Zero-dollar constraint test

Zero cash does not mean zero cost. The scarce inputs are operator hours, attention, credibility, and access to buyers. With ChatGPT Plus/Work, a local computer, browser, coding ability, local n8n, GitHub, existing social accounts, manual labor, and organic publishing, PREY should prefer models that can sell labor or an outcome before funding infrastructure.

| Model | $0 delivery economics | Distribution implication | Evidence fit | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tiny SaaS | Low marginal cost only after discovery, build, hosting, support, and trust | Needs repeatable acquisition and usually many customers | Standardized slices were repeatedly native/free/cheap | **Weak as default** |
| Productized service | Fixed input/output; manual/AI work behind curtain; cash from first job | Enter where buyers already hire or discuss job | Square, KDP, catalogs, subtitles, listings show service spend | **Strong** |
| Concierge service | Maximum learning; poor scale; fastest workflow evidence | Needs a few consenting users, not audience scale | Mission 01 manual-pack designs were never run | **Strongest discovery model** |
| Paid utility | One-time price avoids subscription resistance; support can consume margin | Needs concentrated channel/marketplace | SRT formatter shows low ceiling; free substitutes common | **Selective** |
| Digital product | Near-zero delivery cost; high commoditization risk | Depends on audience/search/marketplace traffic | No owned audience or asset documented | **Unproven** |
| Data/information product | Monetizes curation; value decays without proprietary access | Needs trust and repeat update need | No privileged dataset documented | **Poor now** |
| Automation template | Fast to create; buyer bears setup/breakage | Ecosystem communities/marketplaces | Many jobs already have scripts/native automation | **Weak unless paired with setup** |
| Open source + paid service | Free artifact builds proof; paid setup/adaptation/support | GitHub demonstrates capability | Could fit API/configuration work; exact spend weak | **Moderate** |
| Free tool + paid outcome | Tool attracts users; paid layer delivers correctness/speed | Organic demos/community drops | Fits audits/preflight, but examples were crowded | **Moderate, segment-dependent** |
| Content-led product | Content compounds trust but costs sustained time | Requires audience, expertise, or content engine | Accounts exist; size/fit undocumented | **Unproven** |
| Marketplace utility | Marketplace supplies intent but adds fees/dependency | Best when $0 listing is available | Freelance hires prove demand; zero-fee access not established | **Conditional** |
| Manual audit / done-for-you outcome | No build; charge for clean result | Public proof and inbound offer can precede outreach | Strong for Square; unsafe in regulated/policy-heavy work | **Strong** |

Under a $0-cash, no-audience-proven constraint, concierge and productized service are better instruments for discovering willingness to pay than standalone micro-SaaS. Software should be earned by repeated paid delivery.

## 5 — Research versus reality

| Assumption desk research cannot settle | Cheapest real-world experiment | Decision signal |
|---|---|---|
| Does a low-volume Square shop prefer per-run payout packs to a $40/month tool? | Publish one synthetic before/after demo in one rules-permitting community and owned account; link an inbound fixed-fee offer | 3 qualified responses, 2 anonymized schemas, 1 paid run |
| Does an incumbent satisfy users, or merely exist? | Show exact alternative workflow and ask users to choose incumbent, status quo, or done-for-you outcome | Behavior/file sharing, not compliments |
| Will buyers trust a new operator with files? | Offer local processing, deletion promise, synthetic demo, and header-only diagnostic | Voluntary redacted/anonymized structure |
| Is human judgment a defect or the product? | Fixed-scope manual sample with objective review checklist | Repeat purchase or paid second batch |
| Can organic distribution reach buyers? | One useful demo and one explicit offer with unique form/link | Qualified inbound, not views/likes |
| Is pain frequent enough? | Ask for next scheduled occurrence and offer that dated delivery | Dated repeat run or recurring payment |
| Does specialization beat generic incumbent? | Name one segment, input, output, turnaround, and price | Segment-specific conversion |
| Are communities reachable without spam? | One educational post where rules allow; reply only to opt-ins | Moderator-safe opt-in conversations |

No cold email, LinkedIn Premium, spam, deception, fake proof, fabricated scarcity, or unsolicited file requests are needed.

## 6 — PREY v2

**OBSERVE → FORM BET → CHEAP EXPOSURE → HUMAN SIGNAL → MONEY TEST → BUILD/KILL**

1. **Observe:** start from an owned/reachable surface or existing asset, not the whole internet.
2. **Form bet:** one user, triggering event, input, output, alternative, reason to choose us, price, and distribution surface.
3. **Cheap exposure:** publish a sample/demo and transparent offer. No product build.
4. **Human signal:** collect opt-in replies, workflow artifacts, or booked conversations.
5. **Money test:** request payment for a manually delivered outcome with a date and acceptance condition.
6. **Build/kill:** automate repeated paid steps; kill when users choose incumbent/status quo or will not commit.

### Hard limits before exposure

- Research: **maximum 4 elapsed hours per bet**.
- Sources: **maximum 12** — up to 4 pain, 3 money, 3 incumbent, and 2 distribution/rules sources.
- Candidates: **maximum 3**; select exactly 1 for exposure or kill all 3 with hard evidence.
- Reality point: expose one demo/offer within **24 hours** after limits are reached; no new broad hunt first.
- Pre-payment build: **maximum 2 hours**, only a synthetic sample, mock output, or manual aid. No app, integration, brand system, or backend.
- Human-signal window: **7 days or 100 qualified views**, whichever comes first.
- Ask for money after **3 qualified opt-ins or 2 usable workflow artifacts**. Praise and surveys do not substitute.
- Kill or reform after 0 qualified replies from 100 qualified views, 0 usable artifacts from 3 opt-ins, or 0 paid commitments from 3 qualified offers.
- Build reusable software only after **2 independent paid deliveries** of substantially the same input/output, unless a client prepays development.

PREY v2 records research facts separately from market facts. Feature pages can kill an identical feature; only user behavior can kill positioning, trust, service preference, and willingness to pay.

## 7 — Decision

**STRATEGIC DECISION: RUN MARKET EXPERIMENT**

### Reopen status

- **Square consignor payout packs — REOPEN.** It was never truly killed, has recurring pain, exact spending, objective acceptance checks, a public surface, and a plausible per-run segment beneath a $40/month incumbent. This authorizes one exposure/money test, not a build.
- **YouTube/bodycam SRT correction — REOPEN.** It has strong repeat-spend evidence and was killed mainly because human judgment conflicted with a SaaS-shaped filter. It is eligible for a later service test, but is not the next mission.

Patreon, KDP formatting, and Etsy listing setup remain premature-death findings but are not reopened: current evidence does not beat Square’s recurrence, objective output, price anchor, and tiny manual delivery.
