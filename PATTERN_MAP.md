# PREY Failure Pattern Map

This map derives only from PREY's recorded candidates and missions. Candidate-level evidence and counting definitions are in `GRAVEYARD.md`.

## 1. Exact incumbent coverage can be a hard product death

Payload's current importer, Pixieset's Copy List plus Lightroom search, TeamSnap's flexible importer, Roster's free smallest-label tier, and Superset's native export routes serve substantially the same narrow output.

**Lesson:** verify same user, starting state, exceptions, and accepted output. Exact coverage can kill a product before exposure; it does not automatically kill every adjacent service.

## 2. Pain can expire

Payload's complaint preceded the official importer. OpenRefine's parser failure gained a free workaround and merged fix. Pixieset/Lightroom complaints persisted despite a documented workflow.

**Lesson:** date-stamp the trace and attack it with current first-party functionality before investing another mission.

## 3. Adjacent spend is not exact willingness to pay

GitHub Projects users pay GitHub, BenchApp clubs pay for management software, podcast buyers pay for editorial bundles, and bookkeeping clients pay for judgment and cleanup. None automatically establishes demand for PREY's narrow artifact.

**Lesson:** record exactly what was purchased. Surrounding spend is a clue, not proof.

## 4. One signal is not a recurring market

Uwazi and Mealie each had one feature request; SRT reformatting had one US$7 hire; WooCommerce-to-Amazon had one US$30 hire plus exception-heavy work.

**Lesson:** one issue or transaction proves occurrence, not recurrence, concentration, or useful economics.

## 5. Human judgment may be the purchased remainder

Shopify catalog work includes taxonomy decisions; settlement work includes accounting judgment; chargebacks require case judgment; Etsy listing work includes taste and positioning; bodycam SRT buyers purchase difficult listening and language judgment.

**Lesson:** map the complete paid outcome. When judgment creates value, test a tightly scoped manual service rather than rejecting it for not being clean SaaS.

## 6. Cheap-incumbent bias overreached

Mission 05 found that 19 of 28 original deaths were primarily assigned to the competition-gap gate. Patreon, KDP, Etsy, Square, and bodycam SRT demonstrate the overreach: research weakened generic software, but did not test specialized done-for-you execution. Only Square and bodycam SRT had enough evidence to reopen.

**Lesson:** incumbent existence is strong negative evidence for identical software. Service preference and willingness to pay require behavior.

## 7. Platform and distribution dependency create non-market failure

Cricut carried proprietary-format and enforcement risk. r/EtsySellers blocked tool promotion. Mission 07 hit browser infrastructure failure. Mission 09 hit revoked Upwork access and HTTP 403.

**Lesson:** check surface rules, account access, and dependency early. Classify access failures as execution/distribution blocks, never customer rejection.

## 8. Broad framing erases plausible wedges

Generic bank reconciliation was crowded; noisy bodycam correction had segment-specific spending. Generic consignment software was served; low-volume Square shops might prefer a per-run pack. EPC extraction was paid but too broad and sensitive for a tiny test.

**Lesson:** define one user, event, input, output, exclusions, turnaround, price, and surface before judging the bet.

## 9. Research was mistaken for reality

Before Mission 05, PREY had examined 29 candidates, asked zero target users, made zero offers, and run zero money tests. Experiment #001 later remained unlaunched after an execution block. Experiment #002 became PREY's first live exposure in Mission 10, but it still has no qualified inquiry or payment.

**Lesson:** research can kill an identical feature or unsafe delivery shape. Only behavior can test positioning, trust, service preference, and willingness to pay.

## 10. A bounded manual service is often the cheapest discriminating test

Square became a US$25 manually checked payout pack. Bodycam SRT became a US$20 existing-file correction with objective exclusions and an ambiguity log.

**Lesson:** test the disputed assumption manually. Do not build reusable software before two comparable independent paid deliveries unless a client prepays development.

## PREY v1 versus PREY v2

| Dimension | PREY v1 | PREY v2 |
|---|---|---|
| Loop | Observe pain → research competition → demand wedge → kill aggressively → build after many gates | Observe → form bet → cheap exposure → human signal → money test → build/kill |
| Strength | Adversarial research prevented unnecessary builds | Retains discipline while forcing contact with reality |
| Failure mode | Generic product death became untested market/service death | Noisy exposure, controlled by scope and checkpoints |
| Evidence | Research often drove the final market verdict | Research facts and market facts are separate |
| Judgment | Often treated as a defect | May be the paid product |
| Build gate | Many research gates | Two comparable paid deliveries, absent prepaid development |

V2 must not regress to broad hunts, unlimited research, invented differentiation, or premature software. It must retain V1's exact-incumbent checks, evidence labels, safety boundaries, and willingness to kill stale or commoditized outputs.

## Top five operational guardrails

1. Confirm current exact incumbent coverage, but call it a product kill—not a market kill—unless humans rejected the offer.
2. Identify whether buyers pay for transformation, judgment, speed, trust, or a bundle before defining the candidate.
3. Check one zero-cost distribution surface and its rules/access early; classify access failure separately.
4. After at most 4 hours, 12 sources, and 3 candidates, expose one bounded bet or kill it with hard evidence.
5. Seek a paid manual delivery before software; views, upvotes, inquiries, prepared assets, and competitor spend are not revenue.
