# PREY Protocol V2

This is PREY's canonical operating protocol from Mission 13 onward. It converts the Mission 05 Autopsy and Missions 06–12 into rules. It preserves PREY v1's adversarial incumbent checks, safety limits, narrow scopes, factual ledgers, and willingness to kill weak candidates, while preventing desk research from substituting for market evidence.

## Canonical loop

**OBSERVE → FORM BET → CHEAP EXPOSURE → HUMAN SIGNAL → MONEY TEST → BUILD / KILL**

The loop is sequential as an evidence standard, not necessarily as calendar work. A candidate may move backward for one bounded revision, enter reserve, or end inconclusive. It may not skip exposure and payment evidence merely because research is persuasive.

## 1. OBSERVE

**Purpose:** find a concrete job, pain, workaround, triggering event, or spending trace worth turning into a falsifiable bet.

### Sufficient observation

Observation is sufficient when PREY can identify:

- a specific user or segment;
- a concrete job/problem in a recognizable context or triggering event;
- at least one current trace of pain, workaround, hiring, purchase, or category spend;
- the present alternative, including native/free tools or manual work;
- one plausible, legitimate distribution surface that can be checked early.

Acceptable evidence includes current user descriptions of the job, repeated workarounds, job posts, purchases or price anchors for the same or adjacent outcome, dated feature requests, current first-party documentation, and observable community or marketplace activity. One strong trace may justify forming a bet; it does not prove a market.

Unacceptable evidence includes invented personas, undated anecdotes presented as current, search-result volume alone, generic trend claims, competitor existence treated as buyer demand, adjacent spending presented as exact willingness to pay, or likes/views presented as commercial proof.

**Exit condition:** record enough to complete a bounded commercial hypothesis and a preliminary distribution gate. Do not demand exhaustive market proof.

### Observation record

Record the source/date, exact user and job, triggering context, present workaround, observed spend if any, current incumbent coverage, distribution lead, evidence label, and unresolved uncertainty. Distinguish what was directly observed from what PREY infers.

## 2. FORM BET

**Purpose:** translate the observation into the smallest commercial claim that reality can disprove.

Every bet must state:

> **WHO** has **WHAT job/problem**, under **WHAT context**; PREY offers **WHAT bounded outcome**, for **WHAT test price or range**, through **WHAT plausible distribution surface**.

Also state the input, exclusions, turnaround, acceptance condition, current alternative, reason the user might choose PREY, cheapest delivery method, success signal, stop condition, and largest unknown.

A valid bet is falsifiable. “High-output bodycam channels with an existing machine SRT will pay US$20 for one manually corrected, upload-ready SRT covering up to 20 source minutes through a permitted creator-services surface” is a bet. “Creators need AI” and “businesses need automation” are not.

**Exit condition:** one scoped offer, one test price/range, one reachable surface, and predetermined evidence that would advance, revise, or stop it.

## 3. CHEAP EXPOSURE

**Purpose:** put the actual bounded bet in front of relevant humans before building reusable software.

Use the cheapest legitimate form that exposes the disputed assumption: a manual service offer, public marketplace listing, rules-permitted community offer, concierge test, paid manual deliverable, or another zero/near-zero-build exposure. No channel is universal. The surface must fit the intended buyer and allow the activity.

Exposure must show the real outcome, scope, price or price range, important exclusions, and a credible next step. Synthetic examples must be labeled. Do not use spam, deception, unsolicited file requests, fabricated proof, prohibited promotion, or cold outreach where consent/rules do not allow it.

Before material validation work, answer:

> **Where can this exact offer legally and legitimately reach its target user for approximately US$0, and what current rule/access evidence supports that path?**

Passing the distribution gate requires one named surface, a plausible concentration of the target user, a permitted offer format, available access, and an observable response path. If rules, account access, browser infrastructure, or platform behavior prevents exposure, record **EXECUTION BLOCK**. Do not record a market kill. Seek another in-scope surface only if the mission permits it.

**Exit condition:** relevant humans can encounter and act on the unchanged offer, or a specifically evidenced execution block is recorded.

## 4. HUMAN SIGNAL

**Purpose:** classify behavior without converting attention into false validation.

| Level | Meaning | Examples | What it permits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NOISE** | Attention without evidence of relevant need | Views, likes, upvotes, irrelevant/generic comments, spam | Record only; no validation claim |
| **INTEREST** | A relevant person meaningfully engages with the job or offer | Scope question, fit question, relevant workflow description, voluntary redacted sample/header | Clarify fit; prepare the money ask |
| **BUYING INTENT** | A relevant person takes a step toward ordering | Asks how to start, turnaround, payment, sending the input, or whether their job fits | Confirm scope and request payment |
| **TRANSACTION** | Money is actually committed or paid for the scoped outcome | Attempted purchase, cleared payment, confirmed paid order, paid manual run | Deliver; update commercial evidence |

A generic comment is not automatically interest. A price question is buying intent but not payment. A promised payment is not a paid run. Record qualification and the evidence location. The Mission 11 ledger remains the operational model for timestamped observations.

**Exit condition:** the bounded observation window/checkpoint is reached or behavior advances to a money test. Insufficient measurable exposure is inconclusive, not rejection.

## 5. MONEY TEST

**Purpose:** test willingness to exchange money for the bounded outcome.

Commercial evidence increases in this order:

1. **Asked about price** — buying intent; the buyer has not accepted the offer.
2. **Agreed to scope** — the job and terms fit; payment is still unproven.
3. **Attempted purchase** — transactional behavior, including a documented payment attempt; diagnose payment friction if it fails.
4. **Paid manual run** — cleared payment or confirmed paid order from an independent qualified buyer; strong commercial evidence.
5. **Repeat purchase** — the same buyer pays again for a comparable outcome; evidence of recurrence.

A paid manual run carries substantially more weight than any number of views, likes, upvotes, compliments, surveys, or generic comments. Adjacent competitor revenue is not PREY revenue. Do not invent a universal revenue threshold: use the bet's declared price and scope, and record actual amounts.

**Exit condition:** payment behavior is observed, the predetermined offer checkpoint is reached, or exposure is proven inadequate.

## 6. BUILD / KILL

**Purpose:** make the next investment decision from separated research, behavior, and transaction evidence.

### Decisions

- **BUILD** — reusable software is permitted after two independent paid deliveries with substantially the same input/output expose a repeated automatable job, or when a client prepays explicitly scoped development. Build only the repeated part.
- **ITERATE** — relevant people show interest/buying intent or a paid delivery reveals a bounded fix to price, scope, trust, workflow, or positioning. State one change and run a new isolated test.
- **RETEST** — the hypothesis remains plausible but the exposure was contaminated, technically broken, or did not reach a defensible audience. Preserve the reason and create a fresh bounded test only when authorized.
- **RESERVE** — evidence is promising but timing, access, priority, or capacity does not justify another test now. State the reactivation trigger.
- **KILL** — specific evidence disproves the defined product, offer, segment, economics, safety, or reachability claim. State whether it is a desk-research kill or market kill and do not generalize beyond the tested claim.
- **INCONCLUSIVE** — evidence cannot distinguish offer failure from inadequate exposure, or the research budget expires with a material unknown. State exactly what remains unknown.

One paid run authorizes manual delivery and learning, not automatically a software build. Where manual testing is reasonably possible, a bounded paid manual run should precede reusable software.

### Build ban

PREY must not build reusable software when any of these is true:

- no relevant human has been exposed to the offer;
- there is no buying intent or transaction evidence;
- a manual paid test is feasible but has not occurred;
- the core problem or workflow can still be tested manually;
- distribution is unresolved or only an execution block has occurred;
- the proposed build is mainly a demo, brand system, dashboard, integration, or backend created to avoid asking for money;
- evidence supports only generic engagement, competitor/category spend, or a prepared launch kit;
- fewer than two independent comparable paid deliveries exist, absent prepaid development.

## Desk-research kill rule

A **DESK-RESEARCH KILL** is justified only when current specific evidence makes exposure wasteful, unsafe, or structurally invalid. Valid grounds include:

- a native/free/current solution serves the same narrow user, starting state, accepted outcome, important exceptions, and adoption conditions, with no credible service, specialization, packaging, trust, speed, integration, or segment wedge;
- the achievable price, delivery effort, required cost, or recurrence makes the defined offer structurally incompatible;
- PREY cannot identify a real user and job after the research budget;
- delivery is illegal, unsafe, deceptive, rights-infringing, or otherwise unacceptable;
- every plausible legitimate distribution path is fundamentally inaccessible, not merely temporarily blocked;
- the pain has expired or the proposed exact output is already reliably supplied with no material residual job.

“An identical software product is weak” is a product conclusion. It is not evidence that no manual service or segment opportunity exists. The Graveyard's Payload, OpenRefine, TeamSnap, Pixieset/Lightroom, Superset, and micro-label royalty cases support exact-output deaths; Square and bodycam SRT show why cheap incumbents and human judgment do not by themselves kill a service bet.

A **MARKET KILL** requires a defined offer to receive the test's predetermined meaningful exposure and fail its declared behavioral or payment condition. Platform denial, inadequate reach, or unavailable metrics are not market kills.

## Research budget

**Exact rule:** Before exposure, a bet may use at most **4 elapsed research hours, 12 substantive sources, and comparison of 3 candidates**. At the first limit reached, PREY must **FORM BET**, **KILL WITH SPECIFIC EVIDENCE**, or mark **UNKNOWN / INCONCLUSIVE**; it may not continue broad browsing. If a bet is formed, expose it within 24 hours after the limits are reached unless a recorded safety, permission, or execution block prevents that. Pre-payment creation is capped at 2 hours and may produce only a synthetic sample, mock output, or manual delivery aid—not reusable software.

The limits are maxima, not targets. Stop earlier when the bet or hard death is already clear. A source is substantive only if it changes or supports a required field: pain/job, money, incumbent, safety/feasibility, or distribution/rules.

## Distribution-first check

**Exact gate:** Before substantial validation, name one approximately US$0 surface where the target user is plausibly present, verify that the offer format is currently permitted and accessible, and identify an observable opt-in response path. Failure of rules, account, browser, or platform access is an **EXECUTION BLOCK**, never a market kill.

## Agent spawn protocol

Current verified autonomous agents remain **0**. Narrative roles and mission names are not agents.

An agent is allowed only for a demonstrated recurring operational job with clear inputs, clear outputs, measurable success, meaningful recurrence, a material automation advantage over manual execution, and a bounded, observable failure mode. “It would be interesting,” dramatic framing, or a single unrepeated task is insufficient.

- **SPAWN** — the job has recurred enough to document its frequency and manual burden; the specification, permissions, success measure, cost ceiling, stop control, and owner are recorded.
- **PROBATION** — the agent performs a small fixed number of supervised real runs; every action/output is attributable, reviewable, and compared with the manual baseline. It cannot claim customers, revenue, or autonomy that did not occur.
- **SURVIVE** — probation shows repeatable useful outputs, acceptable error/cost, measurable time or quality advantage, and no unbounded failure. Record the evidence and ongoing review cadence.
- **KILL** — stop and unregister it when recurrence disappears, outputs fail the acceptance measure, cost/risk exceeds the advantage, permissions cannot be bounded, or manual execution is better. Preserve the factual reason.

**Exact gate:** Spawn only after the same bounded operational job has demonstrably recurred, and only when its inputs, outputs, success metric, recurrence, automation advantage, permissions, cost ceiling, and bounded failure/stop mode are written down. Otherwise keep verified agents at zero.

## Experiment isolation

While a bounded experiment is live, keep its price, scope, copy, distribution surface, audience, and decision window unchanged. Change a live test only for a safety/compliance issue, technical breakage, or iteration explicitly declared in its design. Record the intervention and treat materially changed terms as a new test rather than silently pooling signals.

Unrelated internal work may continue if it cannot alter exposure or contaminate interpretation. Do not repost, boost, cross-post, comment merely to stimulate activity, or DM people unless the experiment explicitly authorizes it. Experiment #002 remains governed by its existing 19–26 September 2026 window and Mission 11 observation rules.

## Canonical evidence labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **VERIFIED FACT** | Directly evidenced and reproducible from a named record, artifact, transaction, or observation |
| **OBSERVATION** | What PREY directly saw at a stated time, without causal interpretation |
| **INFERENCE** | A reasoned interpretation derived from facts/observations; could be wrong |
| **HYPOTHESIS** | A falsifiable claim awaiting a defined test |
| **UNKNOWN** | Not established by available evidence; never silently converted to zero or false |
| **MARKET SIGNAL** | Observed behavior from a relevant human, classified as interest, buying intent, or transaction |
| **EXECUTION BLOCK** | Infrastructure, permission, account, access, rules, or platform failure that prevented the intended action; not demand evidence |

Every material ledger entry should include a label, date/time when relevant, source/evidence location, experiment or candidate ID, and a separate interpretation field. Revenue, customers, paid runs, and agent status require verified facts.

## PREY scoreboard specification

Future missions must preserve a factual, deduplicated scoreboard. Unknown values remain unknown; they are not assumed to be zero. Every change cites the mission/evidence that caused it.

| Measure | Counting rule |
|---|---|
| Candidates examined | Distinct identifiable candidate hypotheses; also retain raw examination-record count where useful |
| Desk-research kills | Distinct candidates currently closed without market exposure, under the desk-kill rule |
| Market kills | Defined offers rejected only after their predetermined meaningful market test |
| Execution blocks | Distinct launch/test events prevented by infrastructure, access, rules, or platform failure |
| Reserves | Distinct candidates intentionally paused with a written reactivation trigger; report historical and current separately if needed |
| Experiments prepared | Distinct, test-ready experiments with offer, scope, price, surface, signals, and stop rule |
| Experiments live | Experiments currently exposed and inside their declared observation window |
| Human signals | Qualified INTEREST-or-higher events; keep noise separate and deduplicate people/events |
| Buying-intent signals | Qualified actions toward ordering; exclude generic engagement |
| Paid runs | Independent scoped runs with cleared payment or a confirmed paid order |
| Customers | Distinct independent paying customers; do not equate runs with customers |
| Revenue | Gross currency actually received/confirmed; state currency and do not net or convert silently |
| Verified autonomous agents | Distinct agents that were actually spawned and verified; exclude roles/processes |
| Apps | Customer-usable applications actually built; exclude scripts, fixtures, and demos |
| Dashboards | Functional dashboards actually built; exclude ledgers/tables |
| Software products | Reusable customer-facing software products actually built; exclude manual kits and internal aids |
| Cash spent | Actual additional out-of-pocket cash spent by PREY; state currency |

## Required decision record

At the end of each loop or mission, record: candidate/bet ID; evidence labels and locations; research budget used; distribution-gate result; exposure terms and window; observed signal counts; money result; decision (**BUILD / ITERATE / RETEST / RESERVE / KILL / INCONCLUSIVE**); scope of that decision; next checkpoint; and anything that remained unchanged.
