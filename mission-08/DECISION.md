# Mission 08 — Second Chamber

Date: 19 September 2026

## Verdict

**EXPERIMENT-WORTHY.** This authorizes one manual market test, not software, outreach, or a claim that demand is validated.

## Original evidence recovered

The exact source record is `evidence/follow-the-money-01.md`, section 8.

- **Exact user:** a high-volume YouTube operator publishing interrogation and police-bodycam footage. The original Upwork buyer was a small Australian company with US$32,000 total spend and 181 hires when first recorded (185 hires and 38 active contracts when rechecked on 19 September 2026).
- **Painful job:** take auto-generated English subtitles for long-form interrogation/bodycam footage with poor audio, listen carefully, and correct transcription and spelling errors.
- **Current workaround:** buyer-supplied auto-caption software followed by a fluent-English human. A separate buyer sought manual SRT creation plus proofreading, timing, synchronization, grammar, spelling, and style consistency.
- **Money evidence:** the bodycam buyer offered US$10/video, US$250 fixed-price, with regular and consistent work. The separate manual-SRT buyer had US$157,000 platform spend in the original record. These are observed buyer-side signals, not inferred willingness to pay.
- **Incumbents:** YouTube auto-captions/editor, Descript/Whisper-class transcription, and general human caption services. YouTube says poor sound, overlapping speakers, accents, dialects, and mispronunciations can produce errors and recommends review.
- **Distribution:** Upwork contains both buyer job demand and a browsable Subtitles & Captions Project Catalog.
- **Why reopened:** Mission 04 proved another automatic pass was not a credible product wedge, then treated the human judgment buyers explicitly purchased as fatal. The Autopsy correctly called that an assumed product-model constraint, not a demonstrated market death.

Original sources: [bodycam SRT job](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/SRT-Subtitle-Editor-for-Raw-Footage-long-form-videos_~022098401349035962008/), [manual SRT job](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/English-SRT-Manual-Creation-and-Proofreading-Correcting-Existing-Files_~022097008413134378326/), and [YouTube caption limitations](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6373554?hl=en).

## September 2026 reality check

The generic market is crowded and cheap. Upwork showed 2,008 Subtitles & Captions catalog projects, commonly starting at US$10–$20. YouTube offers automatic captions and editing. Happy Scribe offers AI from free/US$8.50 monthly and human proofreading from US$2/minute; Rev offers free AI minutes and paid plans with human-service discounts. These facts kill a generic “accurate captions” offer.

The bodycam edge remains awkward. On 17 September 2026, a second independent Upwork buyer sought editors for an established police-bodycam YouTube channel. The workflow was AI transcription followed by speaker alignment, error correction, sensitive-word audio cuts and subtitle censorship, then delivery of the project file and export. The buyer offered US$1 per source-video minute, described mildly consistent ongoing work, a 3-day turnaround, more than 30 hours/week, and 6+ months. That is current, segment-specific repetition and spending.

Current sources: [second bodycam subtitle job](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Seeking-subtitle-editors-for-police-body-cam-videos_~022099875845273669767/), [Upwork caption catalog](https://www.upwork.com/services/subtitles-captions), [Happy Scribe pricing](https://www.happyscribe.com/pricing), and [Rev pricing](https://www.rev.com/pricing?tab=transcripts).

## Exact wedge

**Small, high-output English-language police-bodycam/interrogation YouTube post-production teams + noisy or overlapping raw footage with an existing machine-generated English SRT + misheard speech, speaker confusion, mistimed cues, and uncensored sensitive words + a corrected, synchronized, upload-ready SRT with an ambiguity log.**

It excludes translation, legal/evidentiary transcripts, from-scratch caption creation, audio/video censoring, Premiere project files, styling, and publishing.

## Human-judgment test

**A — standardizable enough for a repeatable paid outcome.** Hearing ambiguous speech is judgment, but the acceptance boundary is repeatable: preserve valid SRT structure; correct only against audible English; remove timing overlaps; use `[inaudible]` instead of guessing; label speakers only when supplied or unambiguous; apply one supplied censor list; return a valid SRT and timestamped ambiguity log.

The job becomes bespoke editorial labor if it adds narrative selection, translation, legal-grade certification, speaker research, audio redaction, or video editing. Those are hard exclusions.

## Money and distribution tests

Verified spending: US$10/video with regular work and US$32K buyer spend; US$1/source-minute with ongoing 6+ month demand; an ongoing manual-SRT buyer with US$157K spend; adjacent human proofreading from US$2/minute. Inferred only: that a new provider can win at the proposed price and that the catalog will deliver qualified views.

**Surface:** Upwork Project Catalog → Video & Audio → Subtitles & Captions. Upwork explicitly permits fixed-price listings with scope, price, delivery time, requirements, and samples. Joining and creating projects costs US$0; Upwork charges 0–15% only on earnings. See [Project Catalog rules](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057397533-How-to-create-a-project-in-Project-Catalog) and [freelancer costs](https://www.upwork.com/i/how-it-works/freelancer/).

Competition is severe, but most visible listings are generic. The testable differentiation is the noisy-bodycam starting state, explicit ambiguity handling, and upload-ready correction.
